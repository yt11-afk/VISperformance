The 90-day protocol

Week by week. Pattern by pattern.

Three phases. Each one builds on the last. The architecture is designed so that by day 90, the interrupt isn't a technique you're applying — it's the way you naturally operate.

Midpoint reassessment

Day 45 — You retake the assessment.

Not to grade yourself. To see the data. Your pattern scores at day 45 are compared directly against your original report — showing you exactly where the pattern has shifted, where it's held its ground, and what the second half of the protocol needs to address. This is the moment most clients describe as the first time they believed the work was real.

Phase 3 drill focus

1

Weeks 1–4 · Days 1–28

Recognition — Name the pattern before it names you

▶

The first phase is entirely about visibility. You cannot interrupt a pattern you can't see. Every drill in weeks 1–4 is designed to make the pattern observable in real time — so you catch it in the moment rather than recognizing it three hours later in retrospect.

Week 1

Pattern Mapping

Daily 3-minute log — one moment the pattern fired, what triggered it, what you did. No analysis yet. Just noticing. The goal is raw data.

Week 2

Trigger Identification

Identify the three recurring triggers specific to your pattern. Narrow from "high stakes" to precise: which person, which situation, which internal feeling precedes the loop.

Week 3

Body Signal Awareness

The body activates before the mind knows. Map the exact physical signal — jaw, shoulders, breathing, stomach — that fires 2–3 seconds before the pattern takes over. That signal is your interrupt window.

Week 4

The Quiet Version

Identify where the pattern runs at low intensity — not the detonation, the daily tax. This is the version that costs 15–20% of your performance every day without announcing itself.

◈ Phase 1 milestone: You can name your pattern in real time, within 3 seconds of activation, at least 70% of the time.
2

Weeks 5–9 · Days 29–63

Interruption — Stop the loop before it completes

▶

The second phase installs the interrupt. This is not a breathing exercise you remember in theory. This is a practiced, physical, automatic response that fires from the body signal you identified in Phase 1. Weeks 5–9 are about drilling the interrupt until it becomes reflexive.

Week 5

The Physiological Reset

Your pattern-specific 60-second interrupt, practiced daily in low-stakes contexts. The principle: you rehearse the reset when calm so the nervous system can access it when triggered.

Week 6

Posture and Presence

Every pattern has a physical posture. The Pressure Collapse contracts. The Approval Authority shrinks. This week you build the opposite — the physical architecture of the performer you're becoming.

Week 7

Language Reframe

The belief underneath your pattern speaks in specific language. This week you identify that exact language and install a replacement — not an affirmation, but a precise cognitive reframe that your nervous system accepts.

Week 8

Live Application — Low Stakes

Take the interrupt into real performance contexts — deliberate practice settings where the trigger fires but failure costs little. Tennis drills, practice meetings, controlled pressure scenarios.

Week 9 + Day 45

Midpoint Reassessment

Retake the full 8-question assessment. Receive your comparative report. See the pattern score shift in real numbers. Phase 3 is calibrated to what the data shows.

◈ Phase 2 milestone: You successfully apply the interrupt in a real performance context at least once per week for three consecutive weeks.
3

Weeks 10–13 · Days 64–90

Rewiring — Install the new identity

▶

The final phase moves from managing the pattern to replacing it. The interrupt was the bridge. Rewiring is the destination. This phase builds the identity-level architecture of who you are becoming — not who you're trying to stop being.

Week 10

Identity Statement

Build a precise identity statement calibrated to your specific pattern and your vision of performance. Not an affirmation. A statement of who you already are becoming, grounded in evidence from the past 9 weeks.

Week 11

High-Stakes Application

Intentionally seek the triggering context. Competition, high-stakes meetings, pressure scenarios. Apply the interrupt and identity anchor in real time. Document what changes.

Week 12

Secondary Pattern

Your secondary pattern — identified in the original report — now gets specific attention. Patterns travel in pairs. Addressing only the dominant one leaves the secondary pattern as a back door.

Week 13 + Day 90

Final Reassessment

Retake the assessment. Receive your full 90-day before/after comparison — original scores vs. day 45 vs. day 90. This is your data. This is the evidence that the work was real.

◈ Phase 3 milestone: At day 90, your dominant pattern score has shifted measurably from your baseline. You have a documented before/after report.

Tier III — Inside Out Private

For the performer who needs the full framework applied directly.

Private coaching is not the 90-Day Protocol with more touchpoints. It is a fundamentally different engagement — built around your specific performance context, your specific pattern combination, and the specific high-stakes moments that matter most to you.

Who it's for

The competitive executive tennis player

You compete seriously — club leagues, USTA tournaments, private ladder — and you also run a company, close deals, or lead teams. Your pattern fires in both arenas. The work happens in both arenas simultaneously. The court is the laboratory. The boardroom is the proof.

Who it's for

The high-stakes founder or senior executive

You're at a performance inflection point — a fundraise, a leadership transition, a critical year. The pattern has been manageable until now. The stakes just changed. You need the framework applied to your specific context with precision, not a generic curriculum.

Who it's for

The serious competitive amateur athlete

You play at a level where the difference between your best and your average is almost entirely psychological. You've had technical coaching. You know your strokes. What you haven't had is someone who works on the pattern beneath the technique — the one that fires when the match is close.

The structure

Diagnostic → Custom design → Live integration

Session one is a 90-minute deep pattern diagnostic. Sessions two through twelve are bi-weekly — alternating between pattern work and direct performance integration. Where applicable, on-court sessions bring the framework into real play conditions. Real pressure. Real patterns. Real interrupts.

Investment

Discussed on the call — by application

Private coaching is not right for everyone and not available to everyone. The intake conversation — a 20-minute pattern discussion — determines fit before any investment is discussed. If it's not the right match, you'll know within the first ten minutes.

Availability

Limited to 6 private clients at a time

Private coaching requires real attention. Six clients is the maximum. When those six slots are full, a waitlist opens. If you're considering it, the conversation is the first step — not a commitment.

The pattern conversation

What happens when you book a call.

This is not a sales call. It is a 20-minute pattern conversation — designed to identify where your pattern is currently costing you the most, and which tier of the VIS program is the right fit. You'll leave with something useful regardless of what you decide.

01

The opening question — 2 minutes

Minutes 1–2

Before anything else, one question is asked and listened to without interruption. The answer reveals more about pattern type, transparency-willingness, and transformation-readiness than any intake form.

"Describe a specific recent moment when you performed below what you know you're capable of."

What's listened for: do they describe the internal experience or the external circumstances? Do they say "I" or "they"? Is the moment specific or vague? The answers to those three questions determine the entire direction of the conversation.

02

Pattern confirmation — 5 minutes

Minutes 3–7

If they've already taken the assessment, their pattern is confirmed and deepened. If they haven't, a rapid 4-question verbal assessment identifies the dominant pattern live. Either way, the pattern is named before the call is 8 minutes old.

"What have you already tried to address this?"
"When it fires — what does your body do first?"
"How long have you been aware of this?"
03

Stakes and urgency — 5 minutes

Minutes 8–12

The cost of the pattern and the urgency of addressing it is established — not to create pressure, but to understand what's actually at stake and what the right depth of work is. This determines which tier is appropriate.

"What is coming up in the next 90 days where this pattern will show up?"
"What has this pattern already cost you — concretely?"
"If nothing changes, what does the next year look like?"
04

The right fit — 5 minutes

Minutes 13–17

Based on everything heard, the right tier is recommended — honestly. If the 90-Day Protocol is sufficient, that's what's recommended. If private coaching is the right fit, the structure and investment is discussed. If neither is right, that's said directly.

"Based on what you've shared, here's what I think the right next step is — and here's why."
05

The one thing — 3 minutes

Minutes 18–20

Every call ends with one specific, immediately applicable interrupt — based on the pattern identified in the conversation. Not a teaser. Not a pitch. A real tool they can use before their next high-stakes moment. This is the proof of the framework's value before any payment is made.

"Before we end — here is the one thing you can do in the next 24 hours that will interrupt this pattern."
